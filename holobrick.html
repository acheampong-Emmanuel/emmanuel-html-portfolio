The premise

Standard masonry needs standard bricks.

Most of the world's bricks are not standard.

Bricks that are hand-moulded, sun-dried, field-fired, salvaged or broken behave differently from factory units. In these systems dimensional variation is not a defect; it is the normal condition of the material. Conventional construction resolves it by discarding what does not fit.

Even the standard concedes it. EN 771-1 sorts clay units into tolerance categories T1 and T2, but hand-made brick is placed outside them, under a manufacturer-declared category, because the variation cannot be held.

Background · 01

Nine thousand years of an inexact unit

The brick is one of the oldest manufactured objects still in daily use. Dimensional regulation is the recent part of the story.

Drag the year: see what a brick was at that moment
Four practices, four start dates. Only the last one is about dimension.
·

Brick was sun-dried for roughly three thousand years before anyone fired it. It was then fired for a further six thousand years before anyone regulated its size. Dimensional tolerance is a twentieth-century idea applied to a Neolithic object.

Background · 02

What clay is, and what firing does to it

THE BODY

Alumina and silica with minor lime, magnesia, soda or potash, formed by the weathering of igneous rock. Plastic when wet, hard when dried or fired.

THE COLOUR

Iron oxide at 8–10% gives the pinks and reds. Manganese dioxide at 1–4% gives greys and browns. Nothing is added; the tone is the geology of the pit.

Background · 03

How most of the world's bricks are actually made

Green bricks are stacked into a pyramid over a bed of coal. More fuel is embedded in the units themselves. The stack is lit at the base and the fire climbs. Firing runs up to three weeks; the centre can reach 1400 °C.

Position in the stack is the specification.

The centre of the stack over-fires and warps; the skin under-fires and stays soft. Neither outcome is chosen by the maker; both are decided by where a unit happens to sit in the fire.

Positioning

The gap this project works in

Two regularisations, and this project refuses both.

Digital fabrication regularises the unit and lets the arrangement carry the complexity. Vernacular earth building does the reverse; it accepts an irregular unit and absorbs the variation in a thick joint, a render, or a forgiving geometry.

Here the unit is irregular and the position is specific. What makes that tractable is measuring every unit first.

Two regularisations, and what is left over
Hover a quadrant. Each one is a way of making masonry tractable; only one corner leaves both halves irregular.
4 positions

The claim is narrow on purpose. Mixed reality is not new, and irregular masonry is nine thousand years old. What has not been done is to put the two together, and doing so is what allows a wall to be built from material nobody could have specified in advance.

Precedent · mould-making

3D-printed masters for the Dragon Skin moulds

Each catalogued type is 3D-printed as a solid master, perforations and all. The irregular profile survives from the scan into the print; nothing is squared up on the way.

Dragon Skin silicone is cast around the printed master to form a flexible negative mould. It releases the undercuts that a rigid mould could not, allowing each irregular type to be formed repeatedly.

Circularity

Why this is a reuse argument, not a recycling one

Fired clay has already paid for itself. The question is whether we spend that payment again.

01

The energy is already spent

A fired brick has passed through 800–1100 °C once. Crushing it into aggregate discards that; re-firing pays for it twice.

02

Reuse above recycling

Units are taken as found: not crushed, not re-fired, and with no binder added. Broken and warped bricks enter the wall in the condition the kiln left them.

03

Designed to come apart

Reinforcement threaded through aligned perforations rather than bonded into a cement matrix. Cement mortar is what makes masonry unrecoverable.

04

The inventory is the precondition

You cannot reuse what you have not counted. The scan-and-catalogue step is the circular step.

DONE

Physical survey of existing brick stock, drawn and catalogued.

NOT DONE

No full-height wall in a building: what was built at 1:1 is a section on a bench.

For DiCCRA

What transfers to circular construction in African contexts

01

The survey is the digital step that matters

Circularity begins with knowing what you already have. A phone-based scan of existing stock is a lower-cost, more available entry point than a BIM mandate, and it produces the inventory everything downstream needs.

02

Tolerance can be a design parameter

Hold one dimension, let the rest vary. That single move converts irregular, salvaged and broken material from a rejection problem into a design input.

03

Guidance can be labour-complementary

This is not robotic fabrication: there is no arm and no gantry. The overlay raises what a mason can execute rather than replacing the mason, which matters in contexts where construction employment is the point rather than the cost.

04

Prepare offline, guide on site

Four of the five steps happen before anyone reaches the site. What the site needs is a device displaying an already-prepared model, rather than connectivity or computing power.

WHERE IT IS WEAKEST   The headset. Cost, battery life, legibility in direct sun, tracking on a dusty site, and who owns the brick catalogue once it exists. None of these are solved here.

Discussion

Three questions for discussion

01

Is the headset the wrong device?

It is the binding constraint: cost, battery, sunlight legibility, tracking on a dusty site. The workflow also runs on a tablet. Does that change the economics enough to matter?

02

Who should own a brick catalogue?

Once a stock has been scanned, the catalogue is an asset. If it sits on a European platform, priced in euros, required by a client's reporting: whose asset is it?

03

Does this help the person doing the work?

It raises what a mason can execute. It also adds a device, a dependency and a training requirement. In a sector that is 94.8% informal, is that a net gain for the builder or only for the client?
